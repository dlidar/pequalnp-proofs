# Three-bit unsigned comparison

The assignment asks whether 11 AND, OR and NOT gates suffice. The comparison uses little-endian bits in each three-bit word and is strict. Reference.lean exhibits 12 gates and checks all 64 rows in the project definitions.

## Computational lower bound

If a circuit with at most 11 gates uses three or more NOTs, it has at most eight binary gates. Absorb its inversions into complementary edges and express OR by De Morgan's law. The program live.cpp exhausts these networks on the entire truth table. A frontier contains the signals which still have a reader, including the final output. All six inputs are essential. In a smallest network redundant nodes, constants and copies up to complementation can be removed. At a binary gate the program selects both operands and both input signs. It independently retains or drops each operand according to whether it has a later reader. With t nodes left, at most t+1 signals can still be live. Two assignments of different output must still differ on at least one live wire. These bounds prune the search. There is no eight-node network. The nine-node positive control reaches the comparator.

With at most two NOTs, the 15 increasing chains generated by chains.py impose a different obstruction. The target alternates from zero to one and back three times along each chain. A monotone block can fall only where an input falls. On every chain the two inverter operands therefore have unique traces, in chain order 0000111 and 0011001. One inverter is insufficient. Traces.lean proves the trace constraint and Paths.lean applies it to the 15 paths through the comparator table.

After the second NOT at least two binary gates are necessary. Earlier wires may fall only at the middle descent. Tail.lean checks that neither such a wire, the second inverter output, nor a single binary gate on the resulting pool can have the target trace. Hence an eleven-gate circuit would have at most eight gates up to and including the computation of the second inverter operand, with just one NOT in this prefix. Gates independent of the second NOT may be placed before it in any topological order; the second operand's cone can instead be placed first, with unused work postponed. In these eight slots its final wire may be padded by identity gates. The first NOT must change on the middle descent and is necessary to compute the second driver. Its output and the final prefix output must meet the two trace constraints on 27 distinct inputs.

driver_sat.py checks all eight possible positions of the NOT in this eight-gate prefix. It gives the circuit both constants for free, permits equal operands and ignores unconstrained rows. Each negative result has an independently verified LRAT derivation of the empty clause. A nine-slot positive control computes the complement of the majority of the three right inputs and the parity of these inputs at the required wires. The eight certified exclusions and the positive control give a sharp nine-gate bound for these driver constraints. The certificates can be regenerated with CaDiCaL. two.cpp independently enumerates the two-inverter case. Taken together, these computations give minimum total circuit size 12.

These computational exclusions and the local Lean proofs are fork research. The full Lean proof of the exact assigned negation is a separate verification.

## Replay

Use the project's pinned Lean 4.36.0 environment with the current directory included in LEAN_PATH. Compile Traces.lean before Paths.lean and Tail.lean. Reference.lean is independent. Compile live.cpp with a C++17 compiler and run the executable with budgets 8 and 9. Run driver_sat.py with cadical on PATH. It uses check_rup.py and writes DIMACS and proof files in the working directory containing the script.
